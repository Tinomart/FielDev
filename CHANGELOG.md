# Changelog

FielDev is a fork of FielDes (a field-driven design workbench: a C++ kernel on libfive, a Python library and a Qt application), trimmed for **modelling for game development**.
What FielDes did before the fork is in the git history of this repository (this file used to hold it).

## 1.0.0 (not released)

### Game assets: materials by drag and drop, terrain, levels, props, modular kits
All of this was seen only in a virtual display (software rendering, a Linux machine), not on a real screen and not on Windows; `docs/gamedev.md` says what each is, `docs/gamedev-decisions.md` what was decided and what is open.
- **A card of materials** (*View > Materials*): a swatch for each of the 24 presets, drawn as a ball in its colour, shine and roughness.  **Drag a swatch onto a model** in the viewport, or onto its row in the model tree, and a line is written under the model --
  `rock = with_materials(rock, material_preset("stone"))` --; a second drop on the same model replaces the material in that line; a click on a swatch gives the material to the models that are selected.
- **Terrain** (`terrain`, `.raised`, `.flattened`, `.road`, `.river`, `.cave`, `.add`, `.cut`, `.chunk`, `.textured`, `water`): the ground is a field -- a solid slab under a height made of noise --, so a road keeps its width over any hill, a river's banks slope into the
  ground, a cave is a cut of the solid, and chunks cut from it meet exactly.  Its materials follow its slope and height (grass, rock, sand, snow) and its paths (road, river bed).
- **Levels** (`room`, `corridor`, `doorway`, `window_opening`, `pillar`, `staircase`, `wall_segment`, `build_level`): you say where the empty space is and the walls are what is left round it -- the same thickness everywhere, a corridor that ends inside a room opens into it.
- **Props** (`rock`, `tree` (round, pine, bush), `crate`, `barrel`, `placed`, `merge`, `scatter`): each in its own materials; `scatter` stands them on the ground where it is flat enough and a rule allows.
- **Modular kits** (`kit(cell, wall, floor)`): pieces that end exactly on their cell (floor, ceiling, walls with a door, an arch or a window, a corner, a post, stairs), a plan builder (`build`), `seam` and `check` (what is solid on the face two pieces share).
- `export_mesh(..., model_unit="m")`: what one number of the model is (millimetres by default, as before).- The right-click menu has a **Game** group (under *Parametric shapes*): terrain, room, kit, rock, tree, crate, barrel.  Examples `42_terrain.py`, `43_level_and_kit.py`, `44_props.py`, `45_game_world.py`.
- **Water for a river** (`ground.river_water(0.4)`): a body of its own, in the water material, that fills the channel and follows the river downhill.  **Where a character can walk**: `ground.steep_places(32)` shows the ground in red where it is steeper than 32 degrees (a layer, so it is in an exported file too), `ground.walkable_fraction(32)` is the share of the ground that is not.
  Example `46_water_and_walking.py`.
- **Snow, moss and dust as geometry**: `covered(model, depth=0.1, angle=50, name="snow")` lays a cover on every surface that faces up -- thickest where it faces straight up, thinning out where it would slide off --, with a material of its own that lies only on the cover; it works on props and on kit pieces, and a covered prop can still be `placed`, `merge`d and `scatter`ed; the right-click menu of a model has it too (under *Materials*).
  **Stairs in a kit's plan**: `walls.build(plan, levels=2, stairs={(0, 0, 0): 3})` puts a stairs piece in a cell, leaves out the floor above it and leads up into the next cell; the stairs reach the top of that floor.  Examples `47_snow_and_moss.py`, `48_kit_stairs.py`.
- **Removed**: `export_lods` and the *Levels of detail* entry of the export menu (an engine makes levels of detail); example `37_pivot_and_lods.py` is now `37_pivot.py` and is only about the pivot.

### Every game feature is in the right-click menu; a branch is part of the armature's field
Seen only in a virtual display (software rendering, a Linux machine): the menus were dumped and the text of every new entry was run; nothing was seen in a real window or on Windows.
- **The right-click menu has all of it, in a part of its own.**  The game features are not under Parametric shapes and Operations any more: the places of a game are in **Environment** (New terrain and New water; *Terrain* with *Shape the ground*, *Colour the ground*, *Measure the ground* and *Cut into squares*; *Rooms and corridors*; *Modular kit*; *Props*), as the figures are in Characters, and the materials are in **Materials** (*Give a material*, one entry for each of the 24 materials of the library, by what it is; *Cover the top with* snow, moss, dust or sand).  Every entry has the name the work has and a tooltip that says what it does, in the order the work goes.  Every group of Operations is there once, and the rotations and the arrays stand side by side.  `docs/gamedev.md` says where each is.
- **A branch of an armature is part of the armature, and it drags in real time**: it is blended into what it hangs from in the field -- one surface (a smooth union as wide as the branch is thick), the mesh that is shown when a node is let go and the one that is exported --; while a node is dragged every chain is drawn from its nodes at once, each as a tube of its own, smooth-lit, as a quick picture to follow the hand by (1 to 2 ms per redraw in a virtual display, measured in a drag; the blended mesh takes 70 to 190 ms at that picture's resolution, which is why it is not what is drawn while a node is dragged: decision 17), and the mesh of the field follows within a second or two: the application makes it itself, from the very formula of the field (`smooth_union` of each branch into what is before it), and not by the general mesher of libfive, which took 33 to 61 s for the same armature (0.2 to 0.4 s at 40 samples a millimetre, 1.7 s at 80, in the slow virtual machine; decision 16).  A cross-section that is a primitive, an offset section and a sweep turned or scaled by its gizmo are still meshed from the field by libfive, and are slow there.  Swept meshes are lit with a normal for every corner (the facets of a tube do not show).  `docs/gamedev-decisions.md`, decisions 13 and 16.
- **The coordinates of a piece of a sweep run into the next one along a smooth step** (`_selected_field`, the small field of an armature with branches, and the application's mesh of it), not along a line, so that the surface has no crease where two pieces meet on a bend.
- **A character is meshed in a second or two, not in minutes**: the application reads the armatures of `character(...)` (and a head or a hand that is not one) from the script and makes every level of its mesh from the formula of their fields, mirror image included -- two armatures 0.8 to 1.4 s, mirrored 1.7 s, the character of example 33 1.9 s, in the slow virtual machine, where the field took 450 s and 28 s.  A circle or a polygon as the cross-section, and an armature that is moved, turned or scaled the same in every direction, are meshed that way too; the mesh stops at the region of the scene.  Two characters in a scene no longer take each other's place when the region grows.  Still from the field, and slow: an offset section, a scale of a sweep's own gizmo that is not the same in every direction, a coloured sweep, a part of a character that is a model of more than 4000 terms or without a box.
- **A character, or an armature with branches, that is moved, turned, scaled or reflected as a whole is meshed the same way**: `move`, `rotate_x/y/z`, `scale_xyz`, `scale_x/y/z`, `reflect_*` -- and so `gizmo(...)` with var() numbers and `placed(...)` -- hand the model on to the shape they make, with the transform itself as the three coordinates it makes of x, y and z; the application reads those at four points, at the numbers the var()s have, which gives the matrix and the shift of the placement, reads the field of the model at the mapped point and meshes the box the placement puts it in.  Any number of placements in a row are one.  In a virtual display (four cores, software rendering, 40 samples a millimetre) a mirrored character of two armatures has its last level (a cell of 0.025) in 1.6 s as it is, 1.4 s moved and turned by `gizmo`, 2.4 s scaled by 1.2 and turned by `placed` (a bigger model); each of eight placed armatures (a move, turns about z and about x then y, scales of 1.5, 0.6 and (1, 1.6, 0.7), a reflection, a `gizmo` with var() numbers) took 0.3 to 2.3 s in a scene of sixteen shapes meshed at once; read at the vertices of those meshes, the field of libfive is 3.5e-5 to 8.9e-5 from zero on average.  A placement that is not affine (a taper, a twirl, a shear) is still meshed from the field.  `placed(...)` and `merge(...)` of a character no longer fail (its `_parts` is the list of its armatures, a prop's is a tuple).  Seen only in a virtual display (software rendering, a Linux machine), not in a real window and not on Windows; decision 17.
- **A branch starts flat and its blend begins where it leaves, so there is no bulge behind a junction.**  A branch began as a dome on the node it hangs from, which stuck out of the far side of what it hangs from, and the smooth union added material round that dome: a bulge with a dimple.  Now a branch is cut square at the plane through its node, nothing of it lies behind that plane, and its blend is nothing there and grows to its full width along a smooth step -- in the field and in the application's mesh, by the same law (decision 18).  **A corner no longer swells**: the small field of an armature with branches blended the frame of the next piece into the one before along the whole of a piece, so that a smooth bend has no crease, and at a sharp corner that made the outside of the bend swell (0.8 mm against 0.565 mm to the surface on a node of size 3, measured against the exact field of the same chain); it is blended only where the path bends gently now (fully below 20 degrees from one piece to the next, not at all from 40), and a corner is the mitre it is in the exact field.
- **A small model in a big region no longer makes the fine picture impossible; the armature's mesher is faster.**  The resolution is in samples per mm (40 is a cell of 0.025 mm), and with *Auto scale* on every model is meshed at the scene's resolution scaled by the square root of region over model -- which had no sensible limit (2000 samples across the model).  The test armature at resolution 40 in a region of 100 mm had its node markers and its cut face (about 1.7 mm across) meshed at 310 samples per mm: 1.4 million vertices and 28 to 47 s each, four at once, and the memory passed 6 GB (a watchdog stopped it after 150 s).  That is a coarse picture that comes and a fine one that never does, and a crash on a smaller machine.  The rise stops at about 110 samples across a model now (`Shape::autoResolution`); the scene's own resolution is the floor and is never lowered.  The same scene: 654 MB at most and the whole render 7.7 s; a region of 40 mm 37.3 s before and 8.0 s now, 10 mm 2.0 s and 1.6 s, 3.6 mm 1.9 s and 0.7 to 0.9 s; the blended mesh of the armature itself takes 150 to 290 ms.  **Still 7.7 to 8.0 s in the large regions**, from nine helper shapes meshed in several passes (decision 19).  **The distance to the outline of the cross-section is a grid of cells** (`outline_index.hpp`): the profile showed it was 7.7 of the 10.0 billion instructions of the native mesher (a loop over every segment, for each sample).  It gives the same distance and sign as the loop on nine outlines (a rounded square, two slots, a star, a circle of 300 points, an L, a bow tie that crosses itself, a clockwise outline, a repeated point) and 2 to 3 million points each -- largest difference 1.1e-16, no sign difference more than 1e-9 from the outline -- and is 1.2 to 3.9 times faster; the mesher of the branched armature takes 6.4 billion instructions now (the outline test 2.7), 1.56 times fewer, and 1.2 to 1.6 times less time in the virtual machine, where other shapes are meshed at once.  Its agreement with the field of libfive is as before (the replica of the junction 2.9e-5 on average, none farther than half a cell; the three sized armatures 2.1e-4, 2.9e-4 and 5.2e-4, with 69, 120 and 246 vertices farther).  The tooltip of *Auto scale* says it, in all six languages.  Seen only in a virtual display (software rendering, a Linux machine with four cores), not in a real window and not on Windows; decision 19.
- **The "Rendering" bar does not wait for what nothing paints, and a bar that stays says what it waits for.**  Reported: at the default resolution (10), in the default region, with an armature the program had written click by click, the finished armature was in the picture and "Rendering" stayed up for 20 seconds and more.  It could not be made to stick in a virtual display (the same scene as a file and built by the clicks, slowly and with the clicks 0.3 s apart and drags in a row, one core or four, resolution 5 to 40: every render finished in 0.03 to 0.4 s a click), so what follows is what the logs and the picture show, not a proof.  The triangles in the picture are 0.1 mm -- the cell of resolution 10, on a tube 0.15 mm wide: the armature, made natively, was finished.  What was left to render were the nine points of the scene (its nodes and the corners of its cross-section), balls of 0.33 mm that nothing paints, each meshed by libfive like any other shape: 7 900 vertices each at the default resolution (3.0 s of the mesher in all), and at 40 per mm 88 000 vertices and 4 to 8 s each (52.9 s, 14.5 s on four cores) -- libfive being the one part of the render that is heavy on small allocations, and so the part that can be many times slower on Windows.  **Now:** a point is meshed with 8 samples across its ball (350 to 500 vertices, 20 to 70 ms; 0.5 s of the mesher in all), `View::isRendering` and the bar leave out shapes that are not painted (so a point can never hold "Rendering" up, however slow the machine is; the tour, `Esc` and the test automation wait for the painted shapes only), and a bar that has been up for more than 5 seconds (`FIELDEV_BAR_INFO_AFTER`) writes, above its label, the line of the script of each shape that is not done and the numbers the program keeps of its render, so that a picture of a window that is stuck says what it waits for.  The scene of the report in a virtual display: the whole render at resolution 10 0.9 to 1.0 s before, 0.26 to 0.29 s now; at 40 14.5 s before, 0.47 to 0.5 s now; the region of 100 mm at 40 0.8 s; every step of the live build 0.04 to 0.3 s; the agreement of the armatures with the field of libfive and the regression as before.  **Not seen in the window of the report, nor on Windows**; decision 20.
- **An armature and a character are meshed at the resolution their size asks for, as a primitive is.**  Reported: the resolution at the default (10) is far too low, "contrary to how any primitive renders", which pointed at the scaling.  It was: a primitive is meshed at the scene's resolution scaled by its size next to the region (a ball of 0.15 mm in the default region: 115 per mm at 10), but an armature (and a character), which the application meshes itself at its last level, was meshed at the plain scene resolution because its box was not known -- a cell of 0.1 mm on a tube 0.15 mm wide.  **Now** it is meshed with at least 8 cells across the thinnest cross-section of its sweeps, never below the scene's resolution, and at most 300 samples along the whole model, with *Auto scale* on or off.  In a virtual display (four cores, software rendering): the armature of the report at resolution 10 has a cell of 0.0234 mm (0.1 before), 7 911 vertices (427), the whole render 0.33 s (0.26 s); at 40 0.6 s (0.47 s); the regions of 10 and 100 mm at 40 0.82 s and 0.99 s; the agreement with the field of libfive and the six tests of the branched armature as before.  A ball as wide as the tube is still meshed finer (115 per mm) than the armature (about 43); the number of cells across the thinnest part is one constant (`kCellsAcross`) if that is too coarse.  The default 10 is unchanged (decision 21).  **Not seen in your window, nor on Windows.**

### After a click nothing flickers; a node brings the armature mode back; closing does not wait for a heavy render
- **No flicker on a click (seen in a virtual display with software rendering, not on a real screen, not on Windows).** Four causes, found by recording every frame: (1) the shapes a run replaced were deleted before the new ones had a mesh, so the rings, dots and tube
  vanished for a few frames -- they now stay in the picture until the shape that replaces them has its mesh; (2) the model tree lit shapes by the line numbers of the new text while the viewport still held the old ones, so the selection dropped for up to half a second --
  the selection is kept while the run of an edit is on its way; (3) the first partial result of a run had stale lines and took shapes away that came back a moment later -- they stay until the run is final; (4) a one-node armature is a ball that becomes a tube at the second node.
  A shape that is removed also no longer leaves a dangling hover or press pointer behind.
- **A click on a node of an armature puts the armature mode back on**, going on from that node (a drag still moves it; Shift and Ctrl clicks still select several; `Esc` or a right click ends the mode). Where this is only in the viewport: selecting the node in the tree does not.
- **Closing the window while a heavy model is being meshed no longer waits for the whole render**: the render is told to stop first (example 33 did not exit within 70 s after the window was told to close, and now does).
- Fixed in passing: the timing log (`FIELDEV_TIMING`) read an object after it was deleted and could crash the program once in forty runs.

### The Characters section is its own; a playful look; words a game developer knows
- **New armature is a mode of its own, not the work plane.** *Draw an armature* is gone, and so is the Armature button of the work plane (which is for 2D drawing only: Line, Spline, Point). *Characters ▸ New armature* puts the nodes down **one click after another and writes each into the script as it is put down**: the first click makes the armature (`armature([...], profile=armature_1_cut)`, with its cross-section and its cut face), every click after adds a node (a point with a gizmo, in the list of the call; `profile_edit.py`, op `armature`) in the plane through the node before it that faces the camera, so turning the view puts the next one in depth. **A click on a node goes on from it**; from a node in the middle the armature **branches** (a new armature that begins on the same point, which `skeleton_of` hangs from that joint). A right click or Esc goes back a step -- from the node to none, then out of the mode --, Ctrl+Z takes the last node back, and *Add nodes from here* is in the menu of a node. An armature of one node is a ball with its cross-section (`cut_face` works on it).
- **The Characters menu is made like the others:** *Characters*, with an icon of its own, holds *New armature*, the entry for feature models, *character*, *skeleton_of* and *Reference image ▸*.
- **A playful look that stays a tool.** A new application icon (a friendly block with a face on a sunny tile), a warm cream window with the dark teal cards it always had, rounder and more colourful icons for the kinds of model (with icons of their own for armatures, characters and feature models; none of them red), and friendlier words. The first version of this look -- a lavender window, a violet-to-pink sky behind the models, violet selections -- was too loud for something that is looked at all day and is gone: the background of the viewport is a calm dark slate and the selection is the familiar blue. **Red, orange and green are kept for what the program needs and what is happening** (something is wrong, something waits for you, something is done), and the red, green and blue of the gizmo's axes are kept.
- **Words a game developer knows.** `fillet` is `fill_corners`, `chamfer_union` is `bevel_union`, `thicken` is `solidify`; the menu groups *Offsets and walls* and *Rounding* are *Grow and hollow* and *Round and bevel*; a limb is an armature; the tour's model is a tile with a doorway cut in it, a wall and a peg.
- **More of the library is in the menus.** *Operations* gained *Combining* ▸ smooth_union, smooth_difference, smooth_intersection, bevel_union and morph; *Moving* ▸ rotate_x, rotate_y, reflect_y, reflect_z; *Repeating* ▸ array_polar, array_xyz; a group *Bend and twist* (twist_z, bend_z, taper_xy_z, shear_x_y); a group *Materials* (`with_materials`, with a preset); *Characters* ▸ cut_face. The *Export* menu of a model has *For a game engine*: it writes the `export_mesh` call -- a game-ready glTF, an OBJ in metres with Y up, an exactly symmetric mesh, a rigged export, baked textures, and a *UV map* submenu (box, planar, cylinder, sphere, unwrapped, by armature) -- into the script with the choices written out (`menu_catalog.EXPORTS`). What is still only in the library, by design: the function variants that a menu entry would only repeat (`attract_x` ... `attract_z`), the caches, and the engineering helpers (curve fits, regression).
- **The welcome card** no longer says what FielDev stands for in the words of its parent, and the About text does not name it. The tour has a short Characters chapter: build an armature, branch from a node, the feature models of a face, the body they make.
- **Found by looking at the window, and changed.** The first look at the C++ in a (virtual) window showed: (1) the first click of *New armature* ended in a red error -- the app wrote a numbered `gizmo(...)` line for the new armature, and a gizmo around the whole made it a plain shape that `cut_face` and `skeleton_of` no longer knew; (2) every node put down took 20 to 30 seconds to draw -- the field of a sweep along a spline is slow to clear empty space of, so the time grew with the region of the scene (1.7 s over the armature's own box, 22.8 s over ±10 mm); (3) the tour's *Copy or link* step displayed a model that did not exist, its *Rename* step counted as done when it opened, and a loop in the work plane card ran past its three buttons. What changed: an armature and a feature model are **outlines, as a profile, a polyline or a spline is** (the mechanism of FielDes' last two pushes) -- no gizmo numbers of their own, a gizmo mode in the model tree (`x = gizmo(x, mode='click')`, which returns the model itself), the **shared gizmo of their points** when they are selected as a whole, and their points deleted, hidden and copied with them (`armature._makes_outline_gizmo`, `Shape::setGroupPoints`, `madeOfPoints` in the model tree); the armature's sweep is **placed natively**, as `gizmo()` places a sweep, so that the viewport draws it from its points and does not mesh its field (a sweep that is not made of points is meshed over a cube of its own, `custom_resolution.render_hint_for`); the three mistakes of the tour and the work plane card are corrected.
- **Found by looking at the window a second time, and changed.** (1) A click on a node that was already put down did not go on from it, so a branch could never be started: the fact that a variable is a node of an armature was taken when the node's own statement ran, before `armature([...])` marked it, so the app saw no node there. The facts of the nodes are given when the armature's statement is done (`outline._mark_armature_nodes`); run through the library, a click on node 2 followed by a click in empty space writes `armature_2 = armature([armature_1_p2, armature_2_p1])` with its own cross-section and face. (2) The line at the foot of the picture that said what the clicks do in the armature mode -- permanent explanation text on the screen of a tool, cut off in a narrow view -- is gone, and its two texts with it in the six languages: the tour (*Build a character*, *Branch from a node*) and `docs/characters.md` teach what the clicks do; the ring on the node the next one follows, the dashed line and the dot at the cursor stay. (3) The welcome card said twice that the tour takes a few minutes; the small print says only where the tour starts again (all six languages). (4) The byte-code folders (`__pycache__`) that the app's runs leave were part of the repository; they are not any more, and a `.gitignore` says so. Not a fault: *Guided tour* with a script that has unsaved changes asks whether to save first, and a scripted run waits for the answer. Not seen in a window yet: the click itself.
- **Round ends, joints that grow out, and what a node's size does.** (1) The end of an armature is a **dome** where the node there is a spline point -- every node is one, unless it says `point(..., smooth=False)` -- and the flat face of the cross-section at a corner. The viewport builds the dome from the same numbers as the rest of the sweep (`_sweep_round`, `Shape::setSweepRound`, the rings in `Shape::sweepMesh`), and `path_extrude(..., ends=)` makes the same dome in the field -- the cross-section itself shrinking along a quarter of a circle (a first version blended a ball into the field, which clashed with a cross-section that is not a circle: you saw it on a character at once). Measured in the library on a straight sweep of seven cross-sections, the dome of the default rounded square, a flat rectangle, a triangle, a hexagon and a plus lies inside the cross-section everywhere; a U and an L, whose box middle is outside them, are not followed (the dome covers the notch: 340 and 324 points of 2025 per layer, up to 3 mm out of a U 20 mm wide) -- not worth the effort for a shape nobody models an armature in, so it is a known limit (`KNOWN_ISSUES.md`; a corner node gives a flat end). A character is meshed over a cube of its own, at the scene's resolution, and no longer over the whole region of the scene: four of them side by side needed more than 14 GB at 6 samples per mm over 70 mm, and the app was killed. (2) `character(..., blend="auto")` is the default now, and what *Characters ▸ character* writes: each armature is joined with a blend as wide as it is thick (1.2 times), where it was 3 mm everywhere -- on thin armatures that is a blob. Measured in the library at a branch of two tubes of radius 0.94: no blend puts the inside of the corner 1.33 from the joint, a blend of 0.3 1.43, 0.6 1.54, 1.2 1.75 and 3 2.39 (a web two and a half radii wide). (3) The size of a node is the thickness there exactly (library: nodes of size 1, 2 and 0.5 give 0.937, 1.875 and 0.469); every node in every picture of the window tests so far had size 1, so nothing had shown it. Seen in a window (a virtual display with software rendering, never a real screen): the domes of the default section, a flat rectangle, a triangle, a hexagon, a plus and a U -- the first look found the viewport's dome built from four rings, with big flat facets: it has eight now, and looks smooth in the second look --; two characters (a fixed blend of 3 mm, and "auto") at 6 samples per mm over their own cubes, which rendered in about three minutes here without running out of memory. Seen and not changed: a band round the torso of those characters at its middle node (nodes of size 3, 3.2 and 2.2 mm), probably the change of slope of the size there, since the size runs along straight lines between nodes as for every path extrude -- not checked. Not seen: a real screen and graphics driver, and the Windows build running on Windows.
- **What you asked for after looking at it, and changed (seen in a virtual display with software rendering, not on a real screen).** (1) *Armature mode:* a ring at the cursor shows how thick the next node will be -- as thick as the node before it, for a branch as the armature it grows from -- with the armature through it drawn as the cursor moves; the nodes put down are corners except the first and the last, which are spline points (round ends, sharp bends); a new node is as big as the one before it, and a branch gets the cross-section size of the armature it grows from (it took the size of the click, so it did not meet it). (2) *Rings:* a selected armature shows the ring of every one of its nodes, not only of the selected one, and pressing a ring scales that node. (3) *Square ends:* the first cross-section of an armature is turned across the path (it stayed in its own plane, which cut the end slanted when the path left it at an angle; `path_extrude(..., across=True)`, `cut_face` follows). (4) *The tree:* the face (`x_face = cut_face(x)`) is a child of the armature, not its owner; isolating an armature keeps its points, its cross-section and its face; a profile dropped on the face replaces the cross-section. (5) *Scaling with a node:* `sizing="offset"` (`armature`, `path_extrude`) moves the outline of the cross-section by (scale - 1) times the radius of the widest circle it holds instead of scaling it, so that a thinner U stays inside a thicker one and the round ends erode along the same offset; `"scale"` stays the default until the choice is made on numbers. (6) *Look:* teal and peach (a light aqua window, muted teal glass cards -- greyer and calmer than the menu bar, chosen by you from a montage -- peach where something is selected, and a dusty plum grey behind the model instead of the dark blue slate, which weighed on the eye -- chosen by you from a montage of five; ### Armatures are path extrudes; radius circles; feature models; UV maps by part
- **A hex colour is sRGB.** `material(..., color="#3a6ea5")` read the hex as linear numbers, so it looked lighter in the viewport and in an engine than the same hex in a paint program (the open decision of the known issues). It is read as sRGB now, as everywhere else a hex colour is typed, and converted to linear when the material is made (`materials._rgb`); a colour given as `(r, g, b)` numbers stays linear, as glTF has them. No colour dialog was added: a hex colour is typed in the script, where it reads plainly. Example 34 checks the converted values.
- **An armature is a path extrude with an automatic cross-section.** `armature(points, radii, profile, ...)` sweeps a profile along the Catmull-Rom spline through its nodes (the round cones are gone from it; `chain` still makes them). With no `profile=` the armature makes its own: a **square whose four corners are spline points** (nearly round, the radius of `radii`, 1 by default); with `profile=` any 2D shape, which is swept from the first node wherever it is. The thickness at a node is the size of the profile times the node's size (the gold ring of its gizmo). `radii` is one number, or -- for nodes that are tuples -- one for each node; given with a profile of your own it is refused (a profile has its own size). Every armature has the box it fills (`_bounds`), so a character exports.
- **`cut_face(armature)` shows the cross-section at the first node.** A linked clone of the profile (`instance`), turned the way the armature leaves the node, with a dot for each point of the profile: pull a dot, double click it, or press the outline, and the profile -- and with it the armature -- changes. An armature made from the menu (*New armature*) is written with a profile of its own above it (`armature_1_cut`, four spline points in the plane z = 0), `armature(..., profile=armature_1_cut)`, and `armature_1_face = cut_face(armature_1)` under it. **Dropping a profile on an armature in the model tree** (or on a profile it uses) swaps the cross-section: the dropped one takes the place of the old one in the call (`ScenePanel::planDropOn`; the same for the profile of an extrusion).
- **The ring of a selected node is a circle of the armature's real radius.** The gold ring of a node used to have the same size on the screen for every point. For a node of an armature it is drawn, and pressed, at the radius the armature has there (the profile's radius times the node's size, as a circle round the node facing the camera), the way Dust3D shows it: size a node against the picture you model over by dragging the ring out or in. With several nodes selected each keeps a faint circle of its own (`Shape::armatureFactor`, `View::ringPx`; `armature()` tells its points the number, `_armature_factor`).
- **`feature_model(features, view, mirror, snap, openings, trim, fill_holes, back, thickness, name)`: feature models, for faces and other delicate parts.** The method of Dust3D, made for FielDev's own design (the source of Dust3D was read for its rules, nothing of it is copied as a limit). Paths of points -- `feature(points, closed, mirror, opening)`, `feature_ring(...)`, a polyline, a spline, a profile, a plain list -- are triangulated in the plane of a view (a Delaunay triangulation that every loop edge is forced into, by flipping the edges that cross it): **every point is a vertex of the surface, none is added or moved, and every loop stays an edge loop** wherever the points are dragged. The surface is a mesh, the feature model the exact distance to it (a shell of `thickness` when it is open, a solid when `back=True` closes it with rings that shrink to a point behind it). What Dust3D fixes is an argument here, and each loop can override it: the **mirror** is off unless asked for and may be any plane (`"x"`, `("x", 2)`, `(point, normal)`; open loops are half shapes -- both ends on the plane makes a ring, one end a path, neither a mirror image; `snap` is how near the plane an end must be), the **view** the points are joined in is any of front, back, left, right, top, bottom or a (direction, up), the points keeping their own depth, **openings** (an eye: a closed loop with nothing in it, inside another closed loop) can be switched off for all or decided per loop, `trim` and `fill_holes` are choices, and loops that cross are refused with a message naming them. **New feature model** in the Character section writes half a face (outline, eye, mouth, nose) with every choice written out. `skeleton_of` leaves a feature model out (it is skin); `character` joins it with the rest. This replaces the first version, which lofted tetrahedra between rings of the same number of points. A dragged point changes the surface when the script runs again, not during the drag (the surface is a mesh).
- **`uvs="armature"` / `uv_armature(margin, size)`: UV maps made from the armature.** Every triangle belongs to the armature or feature model whose surface it is nearest to (the fields of the parts are asked at the vertices); an armature is laid out as a tube along its path (u round it, v along it, the seam straight along the back, the rounded ends carried on), a feature model flat as it was drawn (in the picture of its view, the triangles that face away from the viewer an island of their own, seen from behind), a character made `symmetric="x"` has an island for each half (the second mirrored back; a feature model that holds both halves itself is one island). Islands are in millimetres and are not turned when packed (`uvparts.py`; `character()` keeps its parts and its symmetry axis).
- **Checked here, and not:** the library parts were run with the Python library only (the stitching of a synthetic face: no triangle faces the wrong way or overlaps another, every loop edge is there, the eye is an opening, the mirror, the snap, the views, the closed back, crossing loops refused; the field signs of armatures and features; the template of *New feature model* rendered and run; the cross-section at three different armature directions; the UV islands of a synthetic tube and feature mesh); the C++ (radius circles, the drop of a profile, the menu entry) was not compiled, and nothing was seen in a window.

### Copies and the gizmo of an outline (the same code as FielDes' last two pushes)
- **Ctrl+D duplicates every model, and the linked copy of a small shape is drawn.** A linked copy (`instance(model)`) of a circle or any other small shape was not drawn: the copy is made again from the original's shape scaled by the numbers of the original's gizmo, and that lost the box the shape was said to fill -- the app sizes the render of a shape from its box, and a shape that does not know it is meshed at the scene's plain resolution, where a circle of radius 0.25 is a few cells wide (`instances.py` works the box out from the original's, `_placed_box`). A profile, a polyline or a spline was refused (one notice line under the tree), and so were a point, a field, a material: **Ctrl+D clones every model, the same way -- `copy = instance(model)`**: a profile or a line as the same outline through the images of the original's points, placed by a gizmo of its own (`gizmo()` places whatever is made of points -- it makes itself again through the placed points, `_remake`, the way the symmetries do -- so a point dragged, a number typed or a point added in the original changes every clone; before, the clone was a copy of the profile's statements that followed nothing). The clone has its points -- a dot for each, the image of the original's point (`points.image_dot`, the mechanism the images of a symmetry use) -- pressed, dragged and double clicked it is the original's point that changes, and a press on the clone's outline puts a point on the original, taken back through the clone's place (`Shape::editLine`, `editMap`). The same press on the outline of an IMAGE in a symmetry -- which did nothing before, the images were not editable -- puts the point on the original too, and the point pulled out is drawn at once, while it is dragged, on the original and on every clone and image of it (`View::setPullVirtual`: each takes it to its own place, and counts its points the way it has them -- a mirror image runs the other way round). A point, a field or a surface is cloned as the shape it is, a material as its own value (`instance` hands the value back), an import by its parts, and an expression that has no name is named first. A clone whose original had no gizmo yet is given one from zero: a clone with none is the same tree as its original, and the viewport draws one shape for one tree. **Shift+D**, the plain copy, copies a profile or a line **with the points it is made of** (it kept the same points) (`ScenePanel::duplicateModels`, `linkModels`, `plainCopyEdits`, `modelsToDuplicate`). **A point of a clone or of an image is selected like the original's:** a press on its dot selects the point it is the copy of -- the original's row -- and shows that point's whole gizmo (the arrows, the ring that turns it, the ring that sizes a spline point's influence) at the copy that was pressed, taken through the copy's map (`View::applyActiveCopy`, `Shape::activeCopy`); what is dragged on it, or Delete, is done to the original's point, so every copy follows. **A profile, a polyline or a spline drawn while a symmetry is on** is written with its images as `symmetry_image(stroke, symmetry, k)` of the whole stroke when they touch nothing (`symmetries._outline_image`, `symmetry_creation.simple_images`, `menu_catalog._stroke_text`), so a point dragged, pulled out of its edge or deleted in the stroke is done to its images, as the clone's; strokes whose images meet them keep the shared points.
- **Dragging the gizmo of a point, or of a profile, starts at once.** Every number that a drag changed measured and meshed again every point it moved -- 27 to 40 ms each on the main thread in the first step in FielDes -- for a mesh that nothing draws, because a point is shown as the dot of its gizmo. A point that is not painted keeps its numbers now and renders nothing (`Shape::updateVars`).
- **A profile, a polyline or a spline has a gizmo when it is selected as a whole.** It had none -- its points have the gizmos, and a profile could only be moved point by point. Selecting the outline itself now brings its points into the one shared gizmo, selected or not: its **arrows** and its dot move the outline in all three directions, its three **scale knobs** stretch it along an axis, its three **rings** turn it about the axes, and with Shift the plane squares and the one gold ring scale it evenly, all about the middle of its points -- the shape stays the shape, bigger, stretched or turned, and the points' own scale and rotation (the radius of a round corner, the direction a spline leaves a point) are left as they are. Selected as points alone, the rings change each point's own numbers as before. What is written is the points' own `gizmo(...)` numbers, so the outline and its dots never part (`View::groupGizmo`, `View::outlineSelected`). Selecting the outline also gives each of its points that has no `gizmo(...)` line one, as selecting any model does (`prepareSelection`). **The outline's gizmo starts off, and has a mode of its own in the model tree**: a new profile, polyline or spline shows no gizmo until the gizmo button on its row (or `E`) says *click* or *always*; the choice is written as `x = gizmo(x, mode='click')`, a gizmo line without numbers (`gizmo()` returns the outline itself, with its points, face and handles, and the mode as `_outline_mode`, read in `interpreter.cpp` into `Shape::outlineMode`; `View::outlinesWithGizmo`). An outline that is *always* shown has its gizmo while nothing else is selected.
- **Not built or run yet:** this was merged from FielDes' patches into FielDev's trimmed sources in a cloud session with no window; the engineering parts of the patches (analyses, lattices) are left out.

### A Character section: armatures, a character, a skeleton, reference images
- **Right-click → Character** (between *Operations* and *Python*, in the menu of empty space and in the menu of a model): **New armature**, **character**, **skeleton_of**, and **Reference image →** front, back,
  right, left, top, bottom.
- ~~**Armature mode of the work plane** (a fourth button next to Line, Spline and Point)~~ -- taken out again, see *The Characters section is its own* above: click the nodes of an armature one after another over a picture, `Enter` ends it, `Backspace` takes the last node back. Each node is a point with
  a gizmo -- the joint of the skeleton, its scale the thickness of the armature there -- and the armature is `armature([...], radii=..., name=...)`. The work plane is made from the view when the mode starts, so the first click lands on the picture
  you are looking at.
- **`armature`, `character`, `skeleton_of`** (`fieldev.stdlib.armature`): an armature is a swept cross-section along a smooth curve through its nodes, round (round cones, with the thickness following a smooth curve through the nodes'
  thicknesses) or any 2D shape by `path_extrude` (`profile=`, carried to its nodes); `character` joins armatures with a blend and mirrors the other half; `skeleton_of` makes a joint for every node, an armature that starts on a joint of another
  hangs from it.
- **Reference images** (`reference_image(path, plane=..., height=..., center=..., opacity=...)`): a picture standing in the scene behind the models, in the plane of a standard view (a picture reads the right way round in the
  view of its name; the character faces -y, as a glTF made of it faces +z), drawn with its own shader, in perspective -- the camera is free. A script that has nothing but pictures to look at is shown from its render region.
- `docs/interface.md` (*Reference images*, the Character section), `docs/characters.md` (*Building a character in the viewport*), `examples/40_armature.py`.

### UV maps and textures baked from fields
- **UV maps** for `export_mesh(..., uvs=...)`: `uv_box`, `uv_planar`, `uv_cylinder`, `uv_sphere` and **`uv_unwrap`** -- the surface cut into islands where it turns (the cut follows the angle you give, on a smoothed normal), each laid flat
  by a conformal map (planar projection where that is not possible), scaled to one texel size, rotated to its smallest box and packed into the square; a second set (`TEXCOORD_1`) for a lightmap; the picture of the layout (`uv_layout=`).
  `examples/38_uv_maps.py`.
- **`bake(...)`** (`textures=`): every texel of a picture is a place on the surface, and what it is made of is read from the fields there -- the **colour** (materials by region, colours and values that are fields), the **normal map**
  (the exact normal of the field in the tangent space of the mesh: a coarse mesh shaded with it looks like the fine surface), **occlusion, roughness and metalness** (a soft ambient occlusion by tracing the field), with padding
  at the islands' edges. PNG files are written by the library itself (`fieldev.stdlib.pngfile`); a `.glb` carries the pictures inside it, an OBJ names them in its `.mtl`. `examples/39_baked_textures.py`.
- **Exact vertex normals** from the gradient of the field (`normals=`), tangents in the glTF, normals in the OBJ and the PLY.

### PBR materials
- `material(...)`: colour, roughness, metalness, opacity and emission, each a number **or a field**; alpha mode, double sidedness, IOR, transmission, clear coat, sheen, normal scale; `where=` for the region of a material;
  **24 presets** (`material_preset`, `material_presets`). Written as glTF materials with their extensions, and as OBJ + MTL.
- **`with_materials(model, [...])`**: the model shown in the viewport in the colours of its materials (the same layers an export paints, found at every vertex of the mesh; the edge between two is cut as sharp as the mesh is fine), with a
  key of their names; `export_mesh` writes the same materials when it is not given `materials=`. The roughness, metalness, glow, opacity and textures are only in the exported file.

### Rigging
- `joint`, `mirror_joints`, `skeleton_of`; skin weights by the nearest bone with a blend at the joints, **a joint that owns a place** (`influence=` a body or a field: the skin there follows it alone, fading outside it over
  `skin_falloff`), and up to **eight** joints a vertex (`max_influences`, `JOINTS_1` / `WEIGHTS_1`). A rigged glTF has the skin, the inverse bind matrices and the weights. The skeleton is the output of a model that is not animated:
  FielDev writes the bind pose; it has no animation and no morph targets.

### Exporting for a game engine
- `export_mesh`: the unit and the up axis (`units=`, `up=`), a budget of triangles (`max_triangles=`), a mesh that is exactly symmetric (`symmetric=`), the origin where the asset stands (`pivot="base"`), materials
  by region and vertex colours from fields; STL, OBJ, PLY, 3MF, glTF (`.glb`, `.gltf`), FBX, OFF and DXF.
